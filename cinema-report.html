Where to sit: the maths of the best cinema seat

After watching Spider-Man: No Way Home, one question stuck with me: which seat makes the screen look biggest? This report answers it in four steps: a textbook cinema, real Australian cinemas, viewers with different heights and eyesight, and finally a group where everyone is different.

  • DH = x·cos 30° = (√3/2)x and HC = x·sin 30° = x/2, so a = 3 + (√3/2)x and b = x/2 + 1.
  • tan β = (14 − b)/a and tan α = (4 − b)/a. Since θ = β − α, the tan subtraction formula gives tan θ = f(x) = (5√3x + 30) / (x² + (3√3 − 8)x + 48).

  • A 10 cm booster cushion, where the cinema offers one, gives a child roughly the eye height of someone 20 cm taller.

Key takeaways
  • In the textbook cinema, the best seat is 4.89 m from the front row, where the screen fills 51.2° of your view.
  • In real cinemas, sit in the first row where the top of the screen is no more than 35° above your eyes. For a typical adult that is Row D in a standard multiplex and Row R at IMAX.
  • Height matters a little. Shorter people and children look up more steeply, so on the biggest screens they should sit one row further back or use a booster cushion.
  • For a group, find each person’s first comfortable row. Book the furthest back of those: everyone is within their limits there, because moving back only reduces neck tilt and screen size. Give the middle seat to whoever needs the most central view.
  • Your eyes change the answer. Older viewers should sit further back, viewers with low central vision closer, and viewers with tunnel vision or motion sensitivity much further back.
